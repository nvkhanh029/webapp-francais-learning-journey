# Les articles partitifs

## What is a partitive article?

A partitive article (`article partitif`) is used before an **uncountable** noun (food, drink, materials, abstract ideas) to show **a part, an unspecified amount**. The meaning is close to "some" in English.

## Forms

| Noun | Article | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | **du** | du pain, du fromage |
| Feminine | **de la** | de la salade, de la viande |
| Before a vowel / silent h | **de l'** | de l'eau, de l'huile |

> **Tip:** here **du** is not the contraction of `de + le`. It is a partitive article of its own and means "some".

## Uses

1. **Food and drink.**
   - Je mange du pain. (I eat bread.)
   - Elle boit de l'eau. (She drinks water.)
   - Nous prenons de la salade. (We are having salad.)
2. **Other uncountable things.**
   - Il a du courage. (He has courage.)
   - Il y a de la musique ce soir. (There is music tonight.)

## Partitive or definite?

| Article | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| partitive (**du, de la, de l'**) | some, a certain amount | Je bois **du** café. (I drink coffee.) |
| definite (**le, la, l'**) | in general, or something known | J'aime **le** café. (I like coffee.) |

For countable things we use `un / une / des`, not a partitive article: `un croissant`, `des pommes`.

## Negation and expressions of quantity

After a negation and after an expression of quantity, the partitive article becomes just **de** (or **d'** before a vowel).

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| ne … pas **de** | Je ne bois pas **de** café. (I do not drink coffee.) |
| ne … pas **d'** | Il n'y a pas **d'**eau. (There is no water.) |
| beaucoup **de** | J'ai beaucoup **de** travail. (I have a lot of work.) |
| un peu **de** | Un peu **de** sucre, s'il vous plaît. (A little sugar, please.) |
| assez **de** | Tu as assez **de** lait ? (Do you have enough milk?) |
| trop **de** | Il y a trop **de** bruit. (There is too much noise.) |

> **Tip:** remember the pair "negation → de" and "quantity → de". Only an affirmative sentence with no quantity word uses `du / de la / de l'`.
