# Les articles indéfinis

## What is an indefinite article?

An indefinite article (`article indéfini`) comes before a noun to talk about a person or thing that is **not yet identified**: it is mentioned for the first time, or it does not matter which one. The meaning is close to "a / an" or "some" in English.

## Forms

| Noun | Article | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine singular | **un** | un livre, un ami |
| Feminine singular | **une** | une table, une amie |
| Plural (masculine and feminine) | **des** | des livres, des tables |

- Before a vowel, `un` and `des` link to the next word: `un ami`, `des amis`.
- French almost always needs an article (or another determiner) before a noun. The plural **des** (some) is often left out in English.

## Uses

1. **Introducing a person or thing for the first time.**
   - Il y a un chat dans le jardin. (There is a cat in the garden.)
   - J'ai une question. (I have a question.)
2. **Talking about some unspecified people or things (plural).**
   - Elle a des amis français. (She has some French friends.)
3. **Introducing a profession with `c'est` / `ce sont`.**
   - C'est un professeur. (He is a teacher.)
   - But: Il est professeur. (He is a teacher.) No article when you only state the profession.

> **Tip:** after the first mention with **un / une / des**, we usually switch to the definite article. For example: J'ai **un** chat. **Le** chat est noir. (I have a cat. The cat is black.)

## In negative sentences

After `ne … pas`, **un / une / des** become **de** (or **d'** before a vowel).

| Affirmative | Negative |
|---|---|
| J'ai **une** voiture. | Je n'ai pas **de** voiture. |
| Il a **des** frères. | Il n'a pas **de** frères. |
| Elle a **un** ordinateur. | Elle n'a pas **d'**ordinateur. |

Exception: with the verb `être`, the article does not change.

- Ce n'est pas **un** chat, c'est **un** chien. (It is not a cat, it is a dog.)

> **Tip:** after a negation, the idea of "a quantity" disappears and only **de** remains. So `pas de voiture` means "no car at all".
