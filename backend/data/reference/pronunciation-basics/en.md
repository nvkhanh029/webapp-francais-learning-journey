French spelling is far from its pronunciation, but there are many stable rules. This page summarizes the most basic pronunciation rules for beginners. Treat the IPA symbols as the standard; the "Approximate sound" column is only there to help you get started.

## Nasal vowels

Nasal vowels are pronounced with air passing through the nose, and the following *n* or *m* is **not pronounced**.

| Spelling | IPA | Approximate sound | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **an, en** | /ɑ̃/ | like "ahn" through the nose | *enfant* /ɑ̃.fɑ̃/ (child) |
| **on** | /ɔ̃/ | like "awn" through the nose | *bon* /bɔ̃/ (good) |
| **in, ain, ein** | /ɛ̃/ | like "an" in *sang*, through the nose | *pain* /pɛ̃/ (bread) |
| **un** | /œ̃/ | like "uhn" through the nose | *un* /œ̃/ (one) |

## The letters s and c

- **s** between two vowels is pronounced /z/: *rose* /ʁoz/ (rose).
- **ss** is pronounced /s/: *poisson* /pwa.sɔ̃/ (fish).
- **c** is pronounced /s/ before *e, i, y* and /k/ before *a, o, u*: *cinéma* /si.ne.ma/, *café* /ka.fe/.

> **Tip:** *poisson* (fish) and *poison* (poison) differ by one *s*, and the pronunciation differs too: /pwa.sɔ̃/ and /pwa.zɔ̃/.

## Final consonants are often silent

Most final consonants are **not pronounced**: *petit* /pə.ti/, *grand* /ɡʁɑ̃/, *vous* /vu/, *temps* /tɑ̃/.

The final letters **c, r, f, l** are often **pronounced**: *sac* /sak/ (bag), *bonjour* /bɔ̃.ʒuʁ/, *neuf* /nœf/ (nine), *animal* /a.ni.mal/. There are exceptions, such as infinitives ending in *-er*: *parler* /paʁ.le/.

## Enchaînement and liaison

- **Enchaînement.** A pronounced final consonant links to the vowel of the next word: *il aime* /i.lɛm/.
- **Liaison.** A normally silent final consonant is pronounced when the next word begins with a vowel: *les amis* /le.za.mi/, *vous avez* /vu.za.ve/.
- There is no liaison after *et*: *un garçon et une fille*, nothing links *et* and *une*.

## The letter h

The letter **h** is always silent, but there are two kinds:

| Kind | Behaviour | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **h muet** (mute h) | allows elision and liaison like a vowel | *l'homme*, *les hommes* /le.zɔm/ |
| **h aspiré** (aspirate h) | no elision, no liaison | *le haricot*, *les haricots* /le a.ʁi.ko/ |

You cannot guess the kind from the spelling: learn it with the word. Dictionaries usually mark *h aspiré*.

## The r sound

The French **r** /ʁ/ is made at the back of the throat. It is not rolled with the tongue: *rouge* /ʁuʒ/, *Paris* /pa.ʁi/.

## Stress

French does not stress each word as English does. The last syllable of a group of words is slightly longer and higher: *Je parle français* /ʒə paʁl fʁɑ̃.sɛ/.
